Time sync is in `engine/net/replicate` (`ReplicateConfig::sync`, on by default):

- **The server's report.** Each snapshot carries the client's slack: the fewest of its inputs
  left waiting on the server over the last 30 ticks, or -1 when a tick went without.
- **The client's clock.** The client runs its tick `replicate_client_dilation` times as fast,
  within 3%, to hold that slack at zero. After a short window it keeps one input in reserve for
  five seconds.
- **The view delay.** The client reckons the server's tick. It draws the others behind that
  reckoning by the worst of what the last 64 snapshot arrivals needed, easing up quickly and down
  slowly. This replaces the fixed six ticks.

The snapshot header grew by the slack byte, so `net-replicate`'s digests moved. Measured by the
new `net-predict` lab and held by `tests/net_predict_test.cpp`, against the done-when cell:

- **Inputs.** With clocks skewed 2%, an input's p95 to the server's step falls from 367 ms to
  50 ms on `lan` and from 467 ms to 250 ms on `bad`. No input is dropped for a full queue, and
  the dilation stays within its 3%.
- **View delay.** With true clocks the view delay falls from 100 ms to 38 ms on `lan`, and from
  120 ms to 88 ms on `wifi`. The others are drawn past the newest snapshot under 1% of the time
  up to `mobile`.
- **Awful links.** On `awful` the delay rises from 317 ms to 444 ms, and the time they stand
  still falls from 8% to 3%.

How it is measured: the queue is held to its reserve at the worst moment of each half-second
window, which the input p95 above shows. It is not measured against the target tick by tick.
